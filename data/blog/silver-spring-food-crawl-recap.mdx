Silver Spring is well suited to a food crawl because several cuisines and price points sit within a compact, transit-accessible area. The DMV Hub has not yet hosted the crawl described here; this is a planning guide for a future community proposal.

## Start with a small route

Two or three stops are enough for a first attempt. Confirm each venue's current hours, reservation policy, accessibility, dietary options, and ability to accommodate the expected group. Do not assume a restaurant can absorb a large walk-in party.

## Publish the important details

Before collecting RSVPs, share:

- the exact meeting point and named host;
- estimated cost and whether purchases are required;
- walking distance and weather backup;
- accessibility and dietary information;
- the maximum group size; and
- how schedule or venue changes will be communicated.

## Keep participation flexible

Let people join for one stop rather than requiring the full route. Meet in public, encourage attendees to arrange their own transportation, and avoid describing any participant as verified unless a real verification process exists.

## Learn before repeating it

After a pilot, ask attendees what worked and what did not. Attendance, quotes, repeat plans, and outcomes should only be published when they actually happened and people consent to being quoted.
